# Prompt 14 — Evidence-Based Results and Discussion

**Study:** Token-Level Uncertainty as a Predictor of Hallucination in Large Language Models  
**Evidence:** Actual files in `AITR_Prompt13_results.zip` (`results.csv`, `evaluation_audit.json`, `test_length_sensitivity.csv`, `rank_disagreement_examples.csv`, and figures).  
**Status:** Interpretation of executed experiment; no new experiment or external literature verification performed here.

## 1. Research questions and experimental protocol

**Primary question:** Does the 90th percentile of answer-token negative log-likelihood (P90 NLL) identify hallucinated responses better than mean answer-token NLL?

**Secondary questions:** (i) Does the comparison change with answer length? (ii) Does a simple answer-length control provide comparable predictive discrimination?

HaluEval `general_data` supplied 600 sampled responses with labels `0` (non-hallucinated) and `1` (hallucinated). Split sizes: train 420, validation 90, test 90. A public `distilgpt2` model supplied answer-token NLL **surrogate scores**, not the generating ChatGPT model's own probabilities. The candidate methods were mean token NLL, P90 token NLL, and answer-token length. Higher scores were treated as more indicative of hallucination. **Average precision (AP)** was the prespecified primary metric and AUROC secondary. P90 NLL was selected on validation AP in Prompt 12, before evaluating the test set; no test-set threshold tuning was recorded.

## 2. Results directly observed (VERIFIED from Prompt 13 outputs)

The held-out test comprised 90 responses, including 17 positive labels (prevalence 18.89%).

| Method | Test AP ↑ | Test AUROC ↑ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean token NLL (baseline) | **0.22342** | 0.49718 |
| P90 token NLL (selected proposal) | 0.20328 | **0.50443** |
| Answer-token length (control) | 0.17771 | 0.32836 |

- **Primary comparison:** P90 minus mean AP = **−0.02014**. Thus the proposed aggregation did **not** improve test AP, despite its better validation AP (P90 0.2827 vs mean 0.2460).
- **Secondary comparison:** P90 AUROC exceeds mean AUROC by about 0.00725; both values are close to 0.5, consistent with weak ranking discrimination in this particular test sample.
- **Class prevalence:** 0.18889. AP for a constant-score, no-skill ranking is equal to prevalence; both NLL methods have AP above this reference, but the margin is modest. This comparison alone does not establish meaningful generalization.
- **Uncertainty:** Exploratory paired row-bootstrap 95% interval for AP(P90) − AP(mean) = **[−0.10752, 0.05195]**, based on 2,000 resamples. It spans zero; do not claim a statistically established difference. The interval is exploratory and conditional on the 90-row test sample.

## 3. Exploratory answer-length sensitivity

The grouping threshold was the validation median answer length, **118 tokens**, rather than a threshold selected on test outcomes.

| Test subgroup | n | Hallucinations | Prevalence | Mean NLL AP | P90 NLL AP | Length AP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Short (at or below cutoff) | 49 | 12 | 0.24490 | 0.29380 | 0.25885 | 0.18008 |
| Long (above cutoff) | 41 | 5 | 0.12195 | 0.14225 | 0.18304 | 0.24008 |

**Observation:** Mean NLL has higher AP within the short group, while P90 NLL has higher AP within the long group. **Interpretation caution:** The groups have very different positive prevalences (24.49% vs 12.20%) and only five positives in the long group. Cross-group AP comparisons therefore mix ranking performance with prevalence; the subgroup pattern is hypothesis-generating, not a demonstrated length-dependent advantage.

## 4. Possible mechanisms and alternative explanations (INFERRED, not established)

1. **High-loss-token sensitivity:** P90 emphasizes unusually difficult tokens, which could sometimes coincide with unsupported or incorrect content. However, high loss can also arise from rare names, uncommon phrasing, or stylistic choices that are not hallucinations.
2. **Distribution shift / sampling variability:** Validation favored P90, but the small test set did not. With 14 validation positives and 17 test positives, modest changes in ranking can move AP noticeably.
3. **Surrogate-model mismatch:** DistilGPT-2 scored responses generated by a different model. Its token likelihood is not a calibrated measure of the response generator's confidence; this limits mechanistic interpretations.
4. **Length and tokenization:** Answer length and token segmentation may affect NLL aggregation. The current subgroup analysis does not isolate a causal length effect.

None of these explanations was directly verified by the supplied outputs.

## 5. Error and failure analysis

`rank_disagreement_examples.csv` contains **15 rows** with the largest differences between mean-NLL and P90-NLL percentile ranks. These are **ranking disagreement examples**, not text-verified hallucination explanations, because the score CSVs do not preserve the source response text or original HaluEval IDs.

Examples include:

- `test_77` (label 0): mean rank 0.0667 versus P90 rank 0.8111; disagreement 0.7444. P90 assigns a much higher relative risk to a *non-hallucinated* labelled row.
- `test_65` (label 1): mean rank 0.1333 versus P90 rank 0.8000; disagreement 0.6667. P90 assigns a much higher relative risk to a *hallucinated* labelled row.
- `test_87` (label 1): mean rank 0.1111 versus P90 rank 0.6667; disagreement 0.5556.

These examples demonstrate that aggregation changes the ordering, sometimes in helpful and sometimes in unhelpful directions. They do not reveal *why* without retrieving the original response texts and inspecting the underlying tokens.

## 6. Answers to research questions and hypothesis status

| Question / expectation | Assessment |
|---|---|
| P90 NLL improves hallucination detection over mean NLL on the primary held-out AP metric | **Contradicted in this test sample**: P90 AP 0.20328 < mean AP 0.22342. Not evidence that mean NLL is universally superior. |
| P90 NLL improves AUROC | **Small descriptive advantage only**: 0.50443 vs 0.49718, both near chance; reliability unresolved. |
| Performance differs with answer length | **Exploratory indication**, but low subgroup counts and unequal prevalence prevent a firm conclusion. |
| Answer length alone is a strong hallucination predictor | **Not supported** by this test sample (AP 0.17771; AUROC 0.32836 with the prespecified higher-is-riskier direction). |

**Overall conclusion:** In this controlled 90-response held-out evaluation, selecting P90 NLL on validation did not yield a test AP improvement over mean NLL. The experiment supports a careful negative or inconclusive finding, not a performance gain or new-algorithm claim.

## 7. Threats to validity and unresolved evidence

- **Sample size and imbalance:** Only 17 test positives; exploratory bootstrap interval is broad.
- **Source provenance:** Split-specific IDs replaced missing source IDs. Source-record disjointness and original-text alignment were **not independently verified** in Prompt 13, although an earlier normalized-question overlap check was reported in Prompt 09A.
- **Original-row scoring audit:** Not performed; arithmetic smoke audit passed, but all 600 row scores were not independently recomputed.
- **Surrogate uncertainty:** DistilGPT-2 was not the response generator; scores cannot be interpreted as the original generator's internal uncertainty.
- **Benchmark scope:** Single HaluEval subset, one seed/split, one scorer and one aggregation percentile; limited external validity.
- **Method selection:** P90 was selected on validation; test performance must be reported without post hoc selection or tuning.
- **Metrics:** No fixed-threshold F1, precision or recall is claimed; those would require a threshold selected without test data.
- **Resource cost:** No timing, energy or memory benchmark is included in the supplied Prompt 13 ZIP; do not claim measured efficiency differences.

## 8. Three prioritized follow-up experiments

1. **Restore source-level alignment and inspect failures (low–moderate cost; highest information value):** Match split rows to original HaluEval records, verify source-level non-overlap, independently recompute a small stratified sample, and examine response texts/token losses for the largest ranking disagreements.
2. **Repeat evaluation with more stratified seeds or a larger untouched sample (moderate cost):** Keep methods and metrics frozen; report distribution of AP differences and uncertainty across repeated splits, avoiding test-set method selection.
3. **Check scorer dependence (moderate–high cost):** Repeat the same protocol with a more appropriate causal LM, or ideally with generator-aligned log probabilities where genuinely available, to test whether the surrogate mismatch drives the weak discrimination.
